*Chlamydophila pecorum* (also classified as *Chlamydia pecorum*) is a Gram-negative, obligate intracellular bacterium of the family *Chlamydiaceae*, primarily infecting ruminants, pigs, and koalas. It causes a range of diseases including polyarthritis, pneumonia, conjunctivitis, and reproductive disorders in livestock, and is a major threat to koala conservation through urogenital and ocular disease. *C. pecorum* has a reduced genome of approximately 1.1 Mb with a highly conserved chromosomal backbone, though strain-level variation in the plasticity zone and polymorphic membrane protein genes drives host tropism and pathogenic potential.
