*Bacteroides caccae* is an obligately anaerobic, non-spore-forming Gram-negative rod in the family *Bacteroidaceae*, residing as a commensal in the human large intestine. It participates in the fermentation of complex polysaccharides and has been implicated in studies of gut microbiome shifts associated with inflammatory bowel disease and colorectal cancer. *B. caccae* possesses multiple polysaccharide utilisation loci (PULs) and a genome rich in carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes), reflecting its specialised role in dietary fibre degradation in the distal gut.
