*Mycobacterium avium* is a slow-growing, acid-fast bacillus in the family *Mycobacteriaceae*, and the most common non-tuberculous mycobacterium causing disease in humans, particularly disseminated infection in severely immunocompromised individuals such as those with advanced HIV/AIDS. It is ubiquitous in the environment, found in water, soil, and biofilms, and also causes avian tuberculosis in birds and Johne's disease-related subspecies in ruminants (*subsp. paratuberculosis*). The genome of *M. avium* (~5.5 Mb) includes a large repertoire of genes for lipid metabolism, cell-wall biosynthesis, and a pathogenicity island acquired by horizontal transfer.
