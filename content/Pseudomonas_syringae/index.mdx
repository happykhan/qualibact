*Pseudomonas syringae* is a Gram-negative, aerobic rod in the family *Pseudomonadaceae*, and one of the most extensively studied plant-pathogenic bacteria, causing blights, spots, and cankers on a vast range of crop and wild plant species. Pathogenicity is primarily mediated by the type III secretion system, which delivers effector proteins into plant cells to suppress immunity. *P. syringae* is classified into more than 60 pathovars based on host range, and its pangenome reveals an extensive and highly mobile repertoire of effector genes, phytotoxin biosynthetic clusters, and ice-nucleation determinants.
