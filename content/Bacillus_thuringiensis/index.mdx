*Bacillus thuringiensis* is a facultatively anaerobic, endospore-forming Gram-positive rod in the family *Bacillaceae*, belonging to the *Bacillus cereus* sensu lato group. It is distinguished by the production of crystalline parasporal insecticidal proteins (Cry and Cyt toxins) during sporulation and is the most widely used biological pesticide worldwide, with applications in agriculture and mosquito control. *B. thuringiensis* shares a nearly identical chromosomal backbone with *B. cereus* and *B. anthracis*, with its insecticidal phenotype determined primarily by the *cry* genes carried on large conjugative plasmids.
