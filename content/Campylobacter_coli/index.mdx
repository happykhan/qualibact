*Campylobacter coli* is a Gram-negative, microaerophilic, spiral-shaped rod of the family *Campylobacteraceae*, primarily associated with pigs but also found in poultry and other livestock. It is the second most common cause of human campylobacteriosis after *C. jejuni*, causing acute gastroenteritis characterised by diarrhoea, abdominal pain, and fever. *C. coli* genomes are approximately 1.7 Mb in size, and the species shows high rates of inter- and intraspecific recombination, particularly with *C. jejuni*, complicating phylogenetic delineation.
