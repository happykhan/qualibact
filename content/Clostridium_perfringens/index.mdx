*Clostridium perfringens* is a Gram-positive, anaerobic, spore-forming rod of the family *Clostridiaceae*, ubiquitously distributed in soil, sewage, and the gastrointestinal tract of humans and animals. It produces a vast array of extracellular toxins and enzymes, and is classified into seven toxinotypes (A-G) based on the combination of major toxins produced, causing diseases ranging from gas gangrene (clostridial myonecrosis) to food poisoning and necrotising enteritis. *C. perfringens* has a relatively compact genome (~3.3 Mb) for a clostridial species, with many toxin genes carried on large conjugative plasmids that drive considerable strain-level virulence diversity.
