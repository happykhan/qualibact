*Bacillus anthracis* is an aerobic, endospore-forming Gram-positive rod in the family *Bacillaceae* and the causative agent of anthrax, a zoonotic disease affecting herbivores and, occasionally, humans. Infection occurs through cutaneous, inhalational, or gastrointestinal routes, with inhalational anthrax carrying the highest mortality, and the organism is classified as a Tier 1 select agent due to its bioweapon potential. *B. anthracis* is genetically monomorphic within the *Bacillus cereus* sensu lato group, with its principal virulence factors -- the tripartite anthrax toxin and the poly-D-glutamic acid capsule -- encoded on the plasmids pXO1 and pXO2, respectively.
