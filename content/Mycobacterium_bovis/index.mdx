*Mycobacterium bovis* is a slow-growing, acid-fast bacillus in the family *Mycobacteriaceae*, and a member of the *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* complex that is the principal cause of tuberculosis in cattle and a wide range of other mammals, including humans. Transmission to humans historically occurred through consumption of unpasteurised dairy products, and *M. bovis* remains a significant zoonotic concern in regions where bovine TB is endemic. The attenuated strain BCG (Bacille Calmette-Guerin), derived from *M. bovis*, is the most widely used vaccine in history; genomically, *M. bovis* (~4.3 Mb) is nearly identical to *M. tuberculosis* but is distinguished by deletion of the RD4 region and an intrinsic resistance to pyrazinamide.
