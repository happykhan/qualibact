*Salmonella arizonae* (formally *Salmonella enterica* subsp. *arizonae*) is a Gram-negative, facultatively anaerobic rod of the family *Enterobacteriaceae*. It is primarily associated with reptiles, particularly snakes and lizards, and can cause gastroenteritis, bacteraemia, and focal infections in immunocompromised humans, young children, and those with reptile contact. *S. arizonae* is distinguished from other *Salmonella* subspecies by biochemical tests such as malonate utilisation and gelatin liquefaction, and its genome carries pathogenicity islands typical of the *Salmonella* genus.
