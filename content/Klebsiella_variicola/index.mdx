*Klebsiella variicola* is a Gram-negative, facultatively anaerobic, encapsulated rod in the family *Enterobacteriaceae*, originally described as a nitrogen-fixing endophyte of plants and closely related to *Klebsiella pneumoniae*. It is increasingly recognised as a clinically relevant species responsible for bloodstream infections, urinary tract infections, and neonatal sepsis, and is often misidentified as *K. pneumoniae* by conventional laboratory methods. *K. variicola* can acquire carbapenemase and hypervirulence plasmids, and genomic studies have revealed distinct population structures adapted to plant, environmental, and clinical niches.
