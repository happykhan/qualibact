*Escherichia marmotae* is a Gram-negative, facultatively anaerobic rod in the family *Enterobacteriaceae*, originally described from faecal samples of wild marmots and later identified in environmental and clinical specimens. It belongs to the cryptic *Escherichia* clade V and is difficult to distinguish from *E. coli* by standard biochemical testing, requiring genomic methods for reliable identification. *E. marmotae* has been found to harbour Shiga toxin genes and extended-spectrum beta-lactamase genes, suggesting potential clinical and public health relevance.
