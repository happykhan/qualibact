*Mycoplasma pneumoniae* is a cell-wall-lacking, Gram-indeterminate bacterium in the family *Mycoplasmataceae* (class *Mollicutes*), and a leading cause of community-acquired pneumonia in school-age children and young adults, often termed "walking pneumonia." It attaches to the respiratory epithelium via a specialised polar tip organelle containing the P1 adhesin and causes tracheobronchitis and atypical pneumonia, with extrapulmonary manifestations occasionally involving the skin, central nervous system, and joints. The genome of *M. pneumoniae* (~0.82 Mb) is one of the smallest of any free-living bacterium and has served as a key model system for systems biology, with growing concern over macrolide resistance driven by 23S rRNA point mutations.
