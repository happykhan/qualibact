*Ureaplasma parvum* is a cell-wall-lacking bacterium of the class *Mollicutes* and family *Mycoplasmataceae*, characterised by its ability to hydrolyse urea for energy generation. It is a common commensal of the human urogenital tract but is increasingly recognised as a cause of non-gonococcal urethritis, chorioamnionitis, and neonatal respiratory infections, particularly in preterm infants. *U. parvum* has one of the smallest known bacterial genomes (approximately 0.75 Mb), with limited metabolic capacity and extensive reliance on host-derived nutrients, and its four serovars show distinct multi-banded antigen gene repertoires.
