*Mycobacterium intracellulare* is a slow-growing, acid-fast bacillus in the family *Mycobacteriaceae*, and together with *M. avium* forms the *Mycobacterium avium* complex (MAC), the most clinically important group of non-tuberculous mycobacteria. It is a frequent cause of chronic pulmonary disease, particularly in elderly individuals with pre-existing lung conditions, and is found widely in water, soil, and household plumbing. The genome of *M. intracellulare* (~5.4 Mb) shows considerable intraspecific diversity and encodes an array of glycopeptidolipid and lipid biosynthesis genes contributing to biofilm formation and host immune evasion.
