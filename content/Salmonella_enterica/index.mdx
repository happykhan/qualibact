*Salmonella enterica* is a Gram-negative, facultatively anaerobic rod in the family *Enterobacteriaceae*, and one of the most important foodborne pathogens globally, encompassing over 2,600 serovars. Depending on the serovar, it causes diseases ranging from self-limiting gastroenteritis (e.g., serovar Typhimurium) to life-threatening typhoid fever (serovar Typhi), collectively responsible for millions of infections and hundreds of thousands of deaths annually. The *S. enterica* genome features multiple pathogenicity islands (SPIs), prophages, and fimbrial operons that drive host adaptation, with a large, dynamic pangenome reflecting extensive serovar-level diversification.
