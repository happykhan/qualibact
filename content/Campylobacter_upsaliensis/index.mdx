*Campylobacter upsaliensis* is a Gram-negative, microaerophilic, spiral-shaped rod of the family *Campylobacteraceae*, commonly carried in the gastrointestinal tract of dogs and cats. It is an emerging cause of human gastroenteritis and bacteraemia, likely underdiagnosed because standard isolation media containing antibiotics inhibit its growth. *C. upsaliensis* has a relatively small genome and, unlike most pathogenic campylobacters, typically lacks the cytolethal distending toxin (CDT) locus, suggesting alternative mechanisms of pathogenesis.
