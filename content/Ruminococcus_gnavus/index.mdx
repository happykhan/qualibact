*Ruminococcus gnavus* (reclassified into the family *Lachnospiraceae*, though retaining its historical genus name) is a Gram-positive, obligately anaerobic coccus and a prevalent member of the human gut microbiota from early infancy. It has been associated with inflammatory bowel disease, particularly Crohn's disease, where its bloom correlates with disease flares and production of an inflammatory polysaccharide. *R. gnavus* genomes encode a diverse array of glycoside hydrolases and mucin-degrading enzymes, alongside biosynthetic gene clusters for antimicrobial ruminococcins, reflecting its specialised niche at the intestinal mucus layer.
