*Wolbachia pipientis* is a Gram-negative, obligate intracellular alphaproteobacterium of the family *Anaplasmataceae* (order *Rickettsiales*), found as an endosymbiont in an estimated 40-60% of arthropod species as well as in filarial nematodes. It manipulates host reproduction through mechanisms including cytoplasmic incompatibility, parthenogenesis induction, male killing, and feminisation, making it one of the most successful reproductive parasites known. *W. pipientis* has a reduced genome (approximately 1.0-1.7 Mb) with extensive mobile genetic elements, and is being deployed as a biocontrol agent against mosquito-borne diseases such as dengue and Zika.
