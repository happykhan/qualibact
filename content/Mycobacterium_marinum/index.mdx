*Mycobacterium marinum* is a slow-growing, photochromogenic, acid-fast bacillus in the family *Mycobacteriaceae*, naturally found in freshwater and saltwater environments. It causes granulomatous skin infections (fish tank granuloma or swimming pool granuloma) in humans, typically on the extremities following aquatic exposure. The genome of *M. marinum* (~6.6 Mb) is the largest among pathogenic mycobacteria and shares extensive synteny with *M. tuberculosis*, making it a widely used surrogate model for studying tuberculosis pathogenesis in zebrafish and other ectothermic hosts.
