*Pseudomonas protegens* is a fluorescent, Gram-negative rod of the family *Pseudomonadaceae*, originally isolated from the rhizosphere of tobacco and cotton plants. It is a well-characterised biocontrol agent that suppresses plant-pathogenic fungi and oomycetes through the production of antimicrobial compounds including 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol, pyoluteorin, and pyrrolnitrin. *P. protegens* has a large genome (approximately 6.7 Mb) encoding an extensive array of secondary metabolite biosynthetic gene clusters, making it one of the most prolific antibiotic producers in the genus.
