*Mycobacterium africanum* is a slow-growing, acid-fast bacillus in the family *Mycobacteriaceae*, and a member of the *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* complex that causes human tuberculosis primarily in West Africa, where it accounts for up to 50% of pulmonary TB cases in some regions. It is clinically indistinguishable from *M. tuberculosis* infection but tends to progress to active disease less frequently. Genomically, *M. africanum* comprises two lineages (L5 and L6) that are phylogenetically basal within the MTBC, with a restricted geographic range suggesting a distinct evolutionary relationship with West African human populations.
