*Rothia mucilaginosa* is a Gram-positive, coagulase-negative coccus in the family *Micrococcaceae*, commonly found in the oral cavity and upper respiratory tract as part of the normal human microbiota. It is increasingly reported as an opportunistic pathogen in immunocompromised patients, causing endocarditis, pneumonia, meningitis, and catheter-related bloodstream infections. *R. mucilaginosa* characteristically forms mucoid colonies and has a genome of approximately 2.3 Mb, encoding polysaccharide biosynthesis genes that contribute to its mucoid phenotype and biofilm formation.
