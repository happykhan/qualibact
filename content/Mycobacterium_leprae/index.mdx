*Mycobacterium leprae* is a slow-growing, acid-fast, obligate intracellular bacillus in the family *Mycobacteriaceae*, and the causative agent of leprosy (Hansen's disease), a chronic granulomatous infection primarily affecting the skin and peripheral nerves. It cannot be cultivated in axenic media and has an exceptionally long doubling time of approximately 12-14 days. The genome of *M. leprae* (~3.3 Mb) has undergone massive reductive evolution, with nearly half of its sequence consisting of pseudogenes, representing one of the most extreme examples of genome degradation in a human pathogen.
